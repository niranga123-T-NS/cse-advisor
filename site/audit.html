Each figure read from a company report keeps the exact line of the PDF it came from. A figure counts towards a score, a label or a checklist only if a cross-check confirms it. Figures that fail a check are still shown, marked ⚠, and are left out of everything else.

What is checked

  1. Last year's column = last year's report. Each report prints the same period last year next to the current figures. When that matches last year's own report, the row and columns were read correctly. This match is also used to pick the right columns when a row holds several.
  2. Printed % change. Where a % change column is printed, it must agree with the current and prior figures.
  3. Quarter vs year-to-date by arithmetic. Year-to-date = the previous report's year-to-date + this quarter. The year-end figure must equal the annual report's EPS.
  4. EPS = attributable profit ÷ shares in issue (latest four reports).
  5. EPS growth agrees with profit growth.
  6. Net assets per share: the balance sheet's comparative column must equal the figure at the last year-end.
  7. Dividends across a share split. A dividend declared before a split is per old share. When a share has split during the report's year or since, its dividend yield is shown but not used.
  8. Plausibility. Per-share figures are checked against the share price. Full-year EPS more than 13 months old is never used. US-dollar rows are skipped. Long-term EPS growth is only measured across years whose figures link up, so share splits don't distort it.

Share splits found in the prices

CSE's price history is not adjusted for share splits: on the day a 10-for-1 split takes effect the price simply drops by 90%. Each such step is found and the earlier prices are divided by the ratio, so charts, signals, alerts and the forecast are on today's share basis. The ratio is taken from the company's own reports (the change in its share count) or a second price source where possible; otherwise from the price itself, which is approximate.
